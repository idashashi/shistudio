Marketing is
not persuasion,
but connection

Marketing is a system that connects those who need something with those who have it. When it is assembled correctly, there is no need to persuade. Persuasion begins where the system is not assembled, and pressure remains the only available tool.

Marketing has a bad reputation, and deservedly so. For too long it meant one thing: how to make a person buy something they don’t really need at a price above reasonable. Hence the “hard sell”, the irritation with advertising, and the fact that entrepreneurs say the word “marketer” in the tone they use for an unavoidable expense.

But this is a description not of the profession, but of its broken version — the one that appears when there is nothing to sell the product with and all that’s left is to push.

Short answer

Marketing is a system that connects those who need something with those who have it. When it is assembled correctly, there is no need to persuade: a person with a problem finds a solution and buys. Persuasion begins where the system is not assembled — and then pressure becomes the only available tool.

Two views of the same work

Marketing as persuasionMarketing as connection
The goal is to increase conversion at any costThe goal is to bring in those the product is a fit for
The audience is those who are cheapest to reachThe audience is those who have the situation
An objection must be overcomeAn objection must be understood: it is often valid
A client saying no is a failureTurning down a good-fit client is a failure, turning down an ill-fitting one is a saving
Success is growth in the number of dealsSuccess is growth in repeat purchases and the share of sales without advertising
Price is how much you can get away withPrice reflects the value for the chosen segment

The difference is not ethical, but economic. Persuasion delivers one-off deals, a high acquisition cost and clients who don’t come back — because they bought something other than what they needed. Connection delivers repeat purchases and referrals, that is, the only two sources of revenue that don’t require paying for each next client.

Why creation pays off better than pressure

This is not about kindness. Do the math.

A client who bought the wrong thing doesn’t come back, doesn’t recommend you and often leaves a review that costs you the next ones. Each sale to such a client is paid for anew and in full — the acquisition cost isn’t spread over anything.

A client for whom it was a fit buys a second and third time at almost no cost to you, brings friends and is more tolerant of the price, because they compare it not with a competitor, but with how things were before.

The whole difference between a business with twenty percent profitability and a business on the edge in the same niche usually lives here, not in the quality of advertising campaigns.

What an assembled system looks like

Connection is not a stance or a tone. It is a structure of five elements, each of which can be checked.

  1. It is clear who faces the situation. Not a demographic persona, but an event: what happened to the person right before they started looking.
  2. The product solves exactly this situation, not an adjacent one. A product that is “almost for this” forces you to explain — and explaining is where persuasion begins.
  3. The price matches the value for this segment. Not the market average and not what you “can get away with”.
  4. The person finds you at the moment the need arises, not when it is convenient for you to show an ad. This determines the choice of channels — based on the situation, not on trends.
  5. The message is phrased in their words. Taken from correspondence and interviews, not invented in a meeting.

If all five align, a sale becomes a short conversation. If even one breaks, compensation begins: discounts, hard closing, nurture funnels, retargeting those who have already said no. All these tools work, but they are expensive — and they treat the symptom.

A diagnostic question

Ask your salespeople: “At what point in the conversation does it get difficult?”

Difficult at the start — the person doesn’t understand what you are talking about, element 5 or 1 is broken. Difficult on price — element 3 or again 1 is broken. Difficult at the end, when they liked everything — you are talking to a segment for which this is not a priority. Easy everywhere, but few come in — element 4 is broken.

The point where the conversation breaks down points precisely to the element that is not assembled. This is the fastest diagnostic of all that I know, and it is free.

What AI changes here

Today, producing messages costs almost nothing. This means the competitive advantage has moved away from the ability to say things well — everyone can say things well now.

Two scarcities remain. The first is understanding who it is for and why: the model doesn’t have your clients, your lost deals or your margin, and has nowhere to get them from. The second is the willingness to give up segments, channels and products; by design, the model lists possibilities and doesn’t choose.

That is why the order of work is reversed from the usual. Previously, you started with what we say and through which channel, and the question “who for and why” was resolved along the way. Now production is no longer the bottleneck, and choice comes first, execution second. This is what I call next-generation marketing: it’s not about new tools, but about the order having changed.

What it looks like in practice

In practice, this means that before questions about channels and budgets, three things are worked out: who faces the situation, what the person did instead of your product, and whether the economics add up at a price that is reasonable for them.

Sometimes such a review reveals that marketing is not needed at all — what’s needed is to change the product, the price or the segment. This is an inconvenient conclusion for a consultant who was brought in for a promotion strategy, but it is honest, and it saves the client more than it costs.

Marketing that starts with the product and ends with the price, not the other way around, stops being an expense on persuasion. It becomes what it should be — a way to make sure what is needed finds the one who needs it.
