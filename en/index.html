Working rules

What we agree on upfront

01

Midpoint check-in

We always check in at the midpoint. If the findings diverge from the original task, work stops and we re-agree the direction. No surprises at the end.

02

“It’s not a marketing problem” is also a result

Sometimes the audit shows the problem lies in how the business itself is set up. That is a full result: it saves more than it costs. This outcome is agreed in advance and paid for on standard terms.

03

There has to be someone to execute

Before we start, we name the person on your side who will put the results to work. If there is no such person, we take on the ongoing support format. Conclusions without someone to execute don’t turn into money.

04

Pause on silence

If there is no reply for five business days or payment is delayed, work is paused and we let you know.

Honestly

Who we are not a fit for

Better to find out now than in week three.

  • Projects without revenue — except startups at launch. Our work relies on numbers. If the product is already on the market but isn’t selling, there is nothing to measure, and any conclusion remains a matter of taste. For a startup at launch, we gather the numbers ourselves — through market research and a financial model.
  • Those looking for confirmation. We come to test, not to agree. If the decision has been made and you need it justified, we are not the right fit.
  • Working through an intermediary without access to you. If we can’t talk to the person whose business it is, we can’t be accountable for the result.
  • Those who need hands. We don’t set up ads, run social media or design layouts.
  • Expecting revenue within a month. Finding where money is leaking can take a week. Stopping the leak takes at least a quarter.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How is a commercial strategy different from a marketing strategy?

A commercial strategy answers what the business makes money on: unit economics, pricing, revenue model, product line. A marketing strategy answers how to talk about it: positioning, channels, funnel. Marketing is the lower level of a commercial strategy. Developing it for a business with unclear economics is pointless: it speeds up spending, not profit growth.

Why does revenue grow while profit stays flat?

Most often there are three reasons. First: customer acquisition cost grows faster than the average order, so each new unit of revenue brings less margin. Second: the range includes negative-margin items that drag the rest down. Third: prices were not revised as costs went up. All three show up in unit economics if you calculate it by segment rather than as a company average.

How is a consulting agency different from an execution agency?

An execution agency sells capacity: the number of hands, channels and deliverables per month, and is chosen on price per volume. A consulting agency sells judgment: what exactly should happen and why. The executor is responsible for execution, the consultant for the decision. That is why you hire an executor when you know what to do, and a consultant when you don’t — or when previous decisions stopped working.

How can you tell a business needs strategy rather than advertising?

Three signs. First: a bigger ad budget grows revenue but not profit. Second: customers buy once and don’t come back — the problem is the product or price, not traffic. Third: different channels convert equally poorly, which points to the offer, not the channel. If at least two signs match, it is too early to put money into advertising.
